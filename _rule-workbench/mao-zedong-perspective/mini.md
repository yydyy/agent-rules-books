# OBEY Selected Works of Mao Zedong

## When to use

Use when analyzing complex problems, formulating strategy under resource constraints, making trade-offs in competitive or uncertain environments, and organizing collective action.

## Primary bias to correct

Treating all problems as equally important, relying on theory or assumption instead of investigation, and seeking decisive confrontation before building sustainable advantage.

## Decision rules

- Start every analysis by identifying the principal contradiction. Its resolution determines whether secondary problems become manageable.
- MUST NOT make decisions without investigation. No investigation, no right to speak; ground every plan in direct contact with reality.
- When forces are unfavorable, trade time for space. Build strength in overlooked edge areas, validate the model, and expand incrementally rather than attacking the strongest point.
- Unite every force that agrees on the principal contradiction, even if other disagreements remain. Avoid creating unnecessary enemies; isolate only those who must be isolated.
- Maintain independence within alliances. Rely mainly on self-reliance; treat external aid as supplementary. Core capabilities must be owned.
- Collect input from the broad base, refine it into systematic direction, and return it for validation before full execution.
- Despise the opponent strategically—every dominant force has hidden weaknesses in essence—but take every tactical engagement seriously.
- Apply dialectical analysis: every situation contains opposing sides. Good conceals risk; bad conceals opportunity.
- Seek truth from facts. Do not substitute dogma, authority, or wishful thinking for direct knowledge of actual conditions.
- Let design and strategy emerge through practice: test, correct, and refine in cycles rather than committing to untested long-term plans.

## Trigger rules

- When facing multiple urgent problems, pause and reframe until the principal contradiction is identified; do not optimize symptoms.
- When entering a new domain or market, conduct direct investigation before proposing architecture, product features, or go-to-market strategy.
- When a dominant competitor or entrenched system blocks the main path, look for an overlooked foothold where resistance is weakest.
- When an alliance or partnership forms, define the principal contradiction that binds it and preserve independence on core interests.
- When a plan assumes favorable conditions throughout, identify the stalemate or defensive phase and prepare for it.
- When grassroots feedback contradicts leadership assumptions, investigate the feedback before dismissing it.
- When fear of a strong opponent produces paralysis, assess whether the opponent is a paper tiger in essence; if so, restore strategic confidence while preparing each step meticulously.

## Final checklist

- Is the principal contradiction identified, or am I optimizing secondary symptoms?
- Is the plan grounded in direct investigation, or does it rely on assumption and analogy?
- Is there a viable foothold to build from, or am I attacking the strongest point directly?
- Are allies aligned on the principal contradiction, and is core capability self-owned?
- Does the strategy account for a protracted struggle, including defensive and stalemate phases?
- Is there a loop to test the plan in practice before full commitment?
