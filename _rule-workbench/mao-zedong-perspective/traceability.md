# OBEY Selected Works of Mao Zedong

Canonical full source: [full.md](full.md)

## Compression decisions

- The canonical source is `mao-zedong-perspective/mao-zedong-perspective.md`; `_rule-workbench/mao-zedong-perspective/full.md` resolves to that source.
- `mini.md` keeps the core analytical frameworks: principal contradiction, investigation-first practice, protracted/periphery strategy, united front, mass line, paper-tiger dialectic, and self-reliance.
- `nano.md` keeps the smallest always-on reminders: find the principal contradiction, investigate before deciding, build from footholds, and test in practice.
- Expressive DNA, role-play rules, humor style, and biography timeline are intentionally lost; they do not change agent analytical decisions.
- Detailed application examples (startups, career, team conflict) are compressed into trigger rules.
- The ten decision heuristics are merged into decision and trigger rules where they change analysis behavior.

## Mini mapping

Decision rules:

- `M1` Identify the principal contradiction first; its resolution determines whether secondary problems become manageable. Source: `Contradiction analysis` (13-18).
- `M2` MUST NOT make decisions without investigation. Source: `Practice and knowledge` (20-25).
- `M3` When forces are unfavorable, trade time for space and build from edge footholds incrementally. Source: `Protracted strategy` (27-31), `Periphery-to-center strategy` (33-38).
- `M4` Unite forces around the principal contradiction while avoiding unnecessary enemies. Source: `United front` (40-45).
- `M5` Maintain independence and self-ownership of core capabilities. Source: `United front` (40-45), `Decision heuristics` (47-56).
- `M6` Collect base input, refine it systematically, and validate before full execution. Source: `Mass line` (50-55).
- `M7` Despise opponent strategically, take every engagement seriously tactically. Source: `Paper-tiger theory` (57-61).
- `M8` Apply dialectical analysis: every situation has two sides. Source: `Contradiction analysis` (13-18), `Decision heuristics` (47-56).
- `M9` Seek truth from facts; reject dogma, authority, and wishful thinking. Source: `Purpose` (3-10), `Values and anti-patterns` (58-63).
- `M10` Let strategy emerge through practice cycles. Source: `Practice and knowledge` (20-25).

Trigger rules:

- `M11` When facing multiple urgent problems, pause to identify the principal contradiction. Source: `Contradiction analysis` (13-18), `Decision heuristics` (47-56).
- `M12` When entering a new domain, investigate before planning. Source: `Practice and knowledge` (20-25).
- `M13` When blocked by a dominant force, look for an overlooked foothold. Source: `Periphery-to-center strategy` (33-38).
- `M14` When forming an alliance, define the binding principal contradiction and preserve core independence. Source: `United front` (40-45).
- `M15` When a plan assumes only favorable conditions, prepare for the stalemate phase. Source: `Protracted strategy` (27-31).
- `M16` When grassroots feedback contradicts leadership assumptions, investigate before dismissing. Source: `Mass line` (50-55).
- `M17` When fear of a strong opponent produces paralysis, assess whether it is a paper tiger in essence. Source: `Paper-tiger theory` (57-61).

Final checklist:

- The checklist restates `M1` through `M10` as a final scan.
